      How to play

      1. Eat glucose and divide

        Each glucose adds a coccus to the end of the chain and restores homeostasis. The bar drains on its own: without glucose, the chain cannot survive. Hitting tissue walls or your own chain ends the game.

      2. Neutrophil: cuts the chain

        It aims ahead of the head to cross your path. If it touches the middle of the chain, everything behind that point breaks off and is lost. If it touches the head, the game is over.

      3. Macrophage: chases the head

        It follows you in a straight line and gets stuck behind walls. Use turns and obstacles to lose it.

      4. Capsule: invisible for 6 seconds

        Collect capsules on the board and store up to two charges. When activated, phagocytes and antibodies can no longer see the chain and move away from it.

      5. Antibodies: slower movement and wider turns

        They bind to the chain for 8 seconds, slowing movement, requiring one straight step between turns, and speeding up phagocytes. Splitting the chain removes them all at once.

      6. Split: shed cells to escape

        From level 2: shed three cocci from the end of the chain. They become a decoy, stopping any phagocyte that engulfs them for a few seconds. Cooldown: 12 s.

      What the board shows
      Golden sphere: glucose.
      Spinning light-blue ring: a capsule to collect.
      Orange halo: fever zone, faster movement and higher consumption.
      Blue halo: cold zone, slower movement and lower consumption.
      Red discs: red blood cells, neutral obstacles that only block movement.
      Flashing strip: an antibiotic about to activate.

      On a keyboard: ↑ ↓ ← → or W A S D.
      Q capsule · E split · space pause.
      On mobile: swipe on the board or tap “Arrows”.

      Culture: all levels unlocked, no homeostasis drain, and slower defenses. Collisions still count; this mode does not unlock the campaign. Virulent: faster movement, faster defenses, and higher consumption. Records are kept separately.

      Playing as the bacterium is a storytelling device. These rules simplify what happens during an infection; “The science” explains the simplifications and lists the sources.
